## 2. What we may publish

**(a) The fine-tuned ModernBERT v3b weights (`models/final_v3b/`, 598 MB).** *Probably yes.* The base model is Apache-2.0, which allows fine-tuned derivatives to be redistributed under Apache-2.0 with the license and notices kept (and a statement that it was modified). Nothing in the licenses of the *code* and *datasets* we trained on (MIT, Apache-2.0) forbids publishing weights trained on them. **What I cannot settle:** the training data also contains text produced by commercial models (section 3.2), and the terms of those providers may limit what the output can be used for. Until that is read and decided, this audit does not clear commercial use; the weights are released under Apache-2.0 by the decision recorded in section 6, and the model card says the terms for model-generated text are unreviewed (and I would not mark the weights "commercial use cleared").

**(b) The human-labeled test set (363 rows; `data/derived/final*/test.jsonl`, `test_real.jsonl`), which contains τ²-bench conversation text.** *Probably yes for the text we took from τ²-bench, with the MIT notice, and unclear for two things.* MIT allows redistribution of the repository's files, including the result files the conversations come from, provided the notice is kept. The two open points: (i) the conversations in those result files were *generated by* OpenAI and Anthropic models (agents `gpt-4.1`, `gpt-4.1-mini`, `o4-mini`, `claude-3-7-sonnet`, user simulator `gpt-4.1`; D-010, D-018) and Sierra's MIT license cannot grant more than Sierra holds in that output (section 3.2); (ii) the rows contain names, addresses, emails and payment details from the τ²-bench domain databases. I did not verify that every one of them is synthetic (the benchmark is built on simulated customers, which I recorded but did not audit); check this before publishing, in particular the telecom and airline rows. Our own contribution (the labels, the class assignments, the decisions in the review queues, `docs/labeling_guide.md`) is ours to license (decided: CC BY 4.0, section 6).

**(c) The training data (`data/derived/`, `data/kaggle_upload_v3b/`).** *Not as it stands; partly yes.* It mixes: BFCL rows (Apache-2.0, publishable with attribution), CriticTool rows (Apache-2.0 on the card only, 3.1), τ²-bench rows (MIT, 3.2), **synthetic negatives written by gpt-5-nano** (D-009), **localization and second-opinion labels from gpt-5-mini and gpt-5-nano** (D-018), **induced runs in which gpt-5-nano was the agent and gpt-4.1-mini the user simulator** (D-019, D-027), and our own programmatic corruptions and controls (ours). The OpenAI-generated parts and the transcripts of other providers' models are the open question (3.2). The programmatic parts, the human labels and the BFCL rows have no known obstacle. A defensible route is to publish (b) and the labels with the scripts that rebuild the training data from the original sources, instead of the rebuilt training files.

## 3. What is unclear (not guessed)

**3.1 CriticTool.** The only license statement I found is the one-line Hugging Face card (`apache-2.0`). The GitHub repository shows no license (recorded in D-019), and the card has no text, no attribution request and no statement of how the examples were produced (D-019: "how produced is not stated"). Apache-2.0 on a dataset card is what we relied on in D-019; it is an assertion by the uploader, who may or may not be the copyright holder. I would ask the authors, or remove the CriticTool rows (878 rows converted, 439 of them errors, all Class 6; in the final dataset they are in the training and validation splits, D-022) from anything that is published. In final_v1 they were 363 of the 2,366 training errors (D-025).

**3.2 Output of commercial models inside our data.** Not read: the OpenAI Terms of Use and Services Agreement, the Anthropic terms, and the τ²-bench authors' statement (if any) on the use of their result transcripts. I know from memory that terms of this kind have contained restrictions on using model output to develop competing models; I did not verify the current wording, whether a tool-call verifier is "competing" in that sense, or whether the restriction applies to our use. Rows affected: all τ²-bench transcripts (agent and user-simulator turns are model output), our induced runs, every synthetic LLM negative, every LLM-proposed label. This is the main unsettled point for publishing (a), (b) and (c); it needs a read of the current terms, or a lawyer, before any "commercial use" claim.

**3.3 Provenance inside BFCL.** The card says all data is Apache-2.0 and lists user-contributed "live" data in other BFCL versions. We use only the multi-turn categories of v4 (D-009 and `data/build_bfcl_pool.py`); I did not trace the origin of each category. No obstacle found; not audited.

**3.4 Personal data and secrets.** (i) Names, addresses, emails and card-like values in τ²-bench conversations: see 2(b). (ii) The repository has no remote and was never pushed (checked: `git remote -v` is empty). Its history (117 commits, 29 MB packed) still holds `data/derived/induced_runs.jsonl` (37 MB of induced transcripts, D-019), removed from the working tree but not from history. **Publishing the existing repository would publish that file**; publish a fresh history or rewrite it first. (iii) I searched the history of every non-`.jsonl` file for strings that look like OpenAI keys (`sk-...`) and for `OPENAI_API_KEY=` assignments: none found. `.env` is ignored, only `.env.example` is tracked. The `.jsonl` files were not searched; run a secret scanner over the whole history before publishing anyway.

**3.5 Models used as judges.** GPT-5-mini and GPT-5-nano are used as baselines (`eval/judge_new_test.py`); their predictions are in `results/judge_new_test/`. Publishing those predictions is a use of their output, same question as 3.2.
